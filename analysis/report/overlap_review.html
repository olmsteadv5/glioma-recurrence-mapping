How much of the later tumor overlaps the original?

Recurrent enhancing tumor seldom lands on the original enhancing tumor: in the median patient only 18% of it does. But it almost always lands close. In 26 of 40 patients all of it was inside the standard 2 cm plan, and a margin of 5.7 mm would have caught 95% of it in the median patient. The whole abnormality, which includes the FLAIR signal around the tumor, spreads much farther: catching 95% of it took a median margin of 21.1 mm, more than 2 cm in 22 of 41 patients.

How to read it

Enhancing tumor
Bright on post-contrast T1: the active tumor. New enhancement is how recurrence is usually called.
Tumor core
Enhancing tumor plus the necrotic center.
Whole abnormality
Everything abnormal, including the FLAIR signal around the tumor, which mixes swelling with infiltrating cells.
On the original
Share of the later tumor sitting on the same part of the original tumor.
Inside the 2 cm plan
Share of the later tumor inside a 2 cm expansion of the whole original tumor, kept inside the brain.
Margin for 95%
The smallest uniform margin around the whole original tumor that contains 95% of the later tumor.

Keep in mind

  • The later scan is not always a recurrence. The first scan is before surgery and the second is simply the next one on file, 31 to 1010 days later (median 263). Patient 00055, at 31 days, is untreated growth. Treatment details are not in the dataset.
  • The pattern classes are approximate. They are defined against the 60 Gy isodose, which we do not have. The stand-in leaves out the planning margin, so real plans would put more patients in the central and in-field classes.
  • The one distant enhancing case, 00302, sits 84% inside the standard 2 cm plan; it only counts as distant against the smaller 60 Gy stand-in.
  • Volumes are measured on the common template brain, where they run up to 18% larger or smaller than in the patient's own scan. Shares and distances are the numbers to read.
